TL;DR

The problem, my decision, the impact.

Problem

Five incomplete design systems, a single shared documentation, and a one-year gap between mockups and production.

Decision

A shared foundation, joint governance, and request management based on designer capacity.

Impact

Design production dropped from 50% to 10% of the time; testing rose from 0% to 30%.

Context & challenge

One change, multiple products to keep in sync.

As a designer in the Design Studio, my role was cross-functional, spanning several Gares & Connexions products. I helped structure and evolve the design systems behind multiple digital products across the rail ecosystem.

At SNCF, some existing products are continuously improved, while others are undergoing a full technology and visual overhaul. Structuring the design systems brought design practices closer together, made shared elements easier to reuse, and introduced a more iterative delivery process.

Key issues

No shared file structure from one project to the next, and an architecture designed without its readers in mind.

Approach

Five challenges, one system built to last.

Help structure a cross-platform, multi-product design system, and manage change requests based on designer capacity.

  1. Diagnose

    Identify what can be shared and reused from one feature to the next.

  2. Define the strategy

    Structure changes around the specifics of web, Android and iOS.

  3. Organize contributions

    Make functional specifications clear for engineering teams.

  4. Support adoption

    Make shared elements easier to reuse and establish a more iterative delivery process.

  5. Drive adoption

    Lead the rollout of the new design systems with stakeholders.

Key decisions

Four decisions that changed how the team delivers.

Each decision addresses a specific finding and leads to a measurable outcome.

D1

Shared, flexible governance

Finding
No shared file structure, a single documentation for five systems.
Decision
A shared organization adapted to each team's constraints, and a single logic: foundations, atoms, functional blocks, page templates.
Impact
More iterativea faster delivery cycle and easier reuse.
D2

Prioritize based on designer capacity

Finding
16 projects to deliver in parallel across 5 design systems, with 6 designers.
Decision
A management dashboard designed to prioritize change requests based on capacity, with a weekly progress update shared with management.
Impact
16 projectsled with 6 designers, across 5 design systems.
D3

Test solutions with users before development

Finding
Solutions built without being validated against real needs.
Decision
An upfront usability testing strategy, with research shared from the design stage onward.
Impact
0 → 30%of time spent on testing.
D4

AI-assisted, reliable handoff

Finding
A one-year gap between mockups and production.
Decision
Delivery templates, Figma files structured by project, component documentation written with AI under designer supervision, and a Storybook for the teams.
Impact
30 → 5%of time spent on handoff.

The groundwork behind these decisions

Governance & evangelism

  • Accessibility awareness (RGAA, RAAM, audits), product by product
  • Delivery process across project managers, designers and developers
  • Dedicated rituals for cross-team exchange

Component libraries

  • Analysis of logic, states and variants
  • Naming conventions
  • Reviews before every version release, test checklist before publishing

Documentation & contribution

  • Support for testers and project managers
  • Contribution process across designers, developers and project managers
  • Design system progress dashboard
